Dataset for our Presentation and Analyses

We will use the Sleep Health and Lifestyle Dataset on Kaggle for our presentation and analyses.

I picked this dataset because:

  • Personal Relevance: I tend to stay up and sleep late pretty often and sleep less than seven hours every school day
  • Professional Curiosity: I am curious about how some parts, such as occupation or the amount of sleep, affect our health
  • Academic Interest: I am interested in health-related topics whenever I do projects or analyses

Dataset Quick Summary

Created by Laksika Tharmalingam, the Sleep Health and Lifestyle Dataset is a dataset from Kaggle that covers “a wide range of variables related to sleep and daily habits.”

  • 374 rows, according to the CSV file
  • 13 columns:
  • Person ID, Gender, Age, Occupation
  • Sleep Duration, Quality of Sleep, Physical Activity
  • Stress Level, BMI Category, Blood Pressure
  • Heart Rate, Daily Steps, Sleep Disorder

Link: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/uom190346a/sleep-health-and-lifestyle-dataset

Problems

Occupation Impact by Sleep: When dealing with stress, it could disrupt our sleep patterns, such as duration and quality. That applies to different occupations, giving some employees work-related stress and irregular schedules. Knowing which occupations are affected can inform workplace wellness policies.

Question: “Do certain occupations report significantly shorter sleep duration or low-quality scores?”

BMI Category and Sleep Health: While obesity is a known risk factor for sleep disorders, its relationship with perceived sleep quality remains understudied in everyday populations.

Question: “How does the BMI category correlate with self-reported sleep quality and stress levels?”

Cleaning and Converting Data

We clean the file from the missing values and convert some columns to different variable types.

# Check if the CSV file have missing values
sum(is.na(sleepData))
## [1] 0

We need to switch the columns from categorical to factorial using the mutate function. That way, it is easier to do analysis testing, such as visualization.

# Converting categorical variables to factors
sleepData = sleepData %>% 
  mutate(
    Occupation = as.factor(Occupation), 
    BMI.Category = as.factor(BMI.Category))

Occupation Impact by Sleep

We want to know if certain occupations report significantly shorter sleep duration or low-quality scores.

# Creating the occSleep variable
occSleep = sleepData %>% 
  group_by(Occupation) %>% 
  summarize(
    avgDuration = mean(Sleep.Duration), 
    avgQuality = mean(Quality.of.Sleep)) %>% 
  arrange(avgDuration)

Occupation Impact by Sleep (cont.)

We finished adding some stuff for the occSleep variable, and it is time to create a barplot graph for the chosen columns. We use ggplot to make a graph and put the occSleep variable to extract data we made earlier.

# Average Sleep Duration Graph
ggplot(occSleep, aes(x = reorder(Occupation, avgDuration), 
                     y = avgDuration)) + geom_col(fill = "red") + 
  coord_flip() + 
  labs(title = "Average Sleep Duration by Occupation", x = "", 
       y = "Hours") + theme_minimal()

We also made a graph for Average Sleep Quality, similar to what we did.

# Average Sleep Quality Graph
ggplot(occSleep, aes(x = reorder(Occupation, avgQuality), 
                     y = avgQuality)) + geom_col(fill = "blue") + 
  coord_flip() + 
  labs(title = "Average Sleep Quality by Occupation", x = "", 
       y = "Sleep Quality from 1-10") + theme_minimal()

Occupation Impact by Sleep Graphs

  • The first half of these graphs shows surprising similarities, including the y-axis, in contrast to the bottom.
  • One of the differences between the graphs is the occupations: nurse, doctor, manager, software engineer, and teacher switch different positions.
  • Engineers have longer sleep duration and better sleep quality than sales representatives

BMI Category and Sleep Health

We want to know how the BMI category correlates with self-reported sleep quality and stress levels. That is like the first problem, except we do not create a custom variable. Also, instead of a barplot, we use a boxplot graph and add a legend.

# Sleep Quality Graph
ggplot(sleepData, aes(x = BMI.Category, y = Quality.of.Sleep)) + 
  geom_boxplot(aes(fill = BMI.Category)) + 
  labs(title = "Sleep Quality by BMI Category", x = "BMI Category", 
       y = "Sleep Quality (1-10)", fill = "BMI Category") + 
  theme_minimal()
# Stress Level Graph
ggplot(sleepData, aes(x = BMI.Category, y = Stress.Level)) + 
  geom_boxplot(aes(fill = BMI.Category)) + 
  labs(title = "Stress Level by BMI Category", x = "BMI Category", 
       y = "Stress Level (1-10)", fill = "BMI Category") + 
  theme_minimal()

BMI Category and Sleep Health Graphs

  • The x-axis shows different categories: normal, normal weight, obese, and overweight. Each has varied colors, which you can see on the legend.
  • The normal BMI categories have better sleep quality and lower stress level than the overweight and obese categories in terms of medians and interquartile range

Conclusion

Occupation Impact on Sleep

  • Engineers have the highest sleep duration (8 hours) and best sleep quality (8.5/10)
  • Sales Representatives have the lowest sleep duration (around 5.8 hours) and poorest sleep quality (4/10), suggesting high work-related stress
  • Actionable insight: High-stress occupations, such as sales or scientists, may benefit from workplace wellness programs to improve sleep

BMI Category and Sleep Health

  • Employees with normal and normal weight BMI report better sleep quality (Median: 8/10) and lower stress (Median: 5-6/10)
  • Employees with overweight and obese show poorer sleep quality (Median: 6-7/10) and higher stress (Median: 6-7)
  • Actionable insight: Weight management initiatives could enhance both sleep and mental health

Limitations and Future Work

Limitations

  • Possible biases in self-reported data
  • Uneven sample size per occupation and BMI group

Future Work

  • Investigate specific interventions
  • Examples: Flexible schedules for nurses, stress workshops for the sales team, etc.
